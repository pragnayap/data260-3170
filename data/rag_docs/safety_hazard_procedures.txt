BAYWOOD TRANSIT AUTHORITY
Safety Hazard Identification and Response Procedures (Procedure No. BTA-SAF-310)
Effective 1 January 2025

1. SCOPE

This procedure governs how Baywood Transit Authority identifies, classifies,
and resolves safety hazards reported under the Safety Hazard incident
category. A hazard is any condition with the potential to cause injury,
property damage, or service disruption, whether or not an injury has
actually occurred yet.

2. HAZARD SEVERITY LEVELS

Every hazard reported to the Safety Office is assigned one of four severity
levels at the time of triage:

  - Critical: an imminent risk to life or an active structural failure,
    such as an exposed electrical conductor, a collapsed platform section,
    or a rail with a visible fracture.
  - High: a serious but not immediately life-threatening condition, such as
    a malfunctioning door that can trap a passenger, or a station stairway
    with a broken handrail.
  - Medium: a condition that increases risk over time if unaddressed, such
    as worn platform edge tiles or a flickering station light.
  - Low: a minor condition with negligible safety impact, such as cosmetic
    damage to a station bench.

3. DISCOVERY-TO-REPORT WINDOW FOR CRITICAL HAZARDS

Any hazard assessed as Critical must be reported to the Safety Office within
2 hours of the hazard being discovered, not from when a formal report is
filed in the incident system. This discovery-based clock is distinct from
the report-filing-based escalation described in the Incident Reporting
Policy, and Safety Office staff are trained to use the earlier of the two
timestamps when both apply to the same event.

4. RESOLUTION TIMELINES

Once triaged, hazards must be resolved or mitigated within the following
windows: Critical hazards within 24 hours of discovery; High hazards within
5 business days; Medium hazards within 30 calendar days; Low hazards within
90 calendar days or at the next scheduled maintenance visit, whichever comes
first. "Mitigated" means a temporary control, such as a barrier or reduced
speed order, has been put in place even if full repair takes longer.

5. REPEAT HAZARD TRACKING

If the same hazard location generates 2 or more Medium or higher severity
reports within any 180-day period, the location is placed on the Repeat
Hazard Watch List and receives a full engineering inspection regardless of
the current severity of the most recent report.

6. ESCALATION BEYOND THE SAFETY OFFICE

Critical hazards that remain unresolved 24 hours after discovery are
automatically escalated to the Accountable Executive, who must either
authorize emergency repair funding or order the affected route segment
closed until the hazard is mitigated.
