BAYWOOD TRANSIT AUTHORITY
Mechanical Failure and Preventive Maintenance Standard (Standard No. BTA-MNT-150)
Revised June 2025

1. PURPOSE

This standard sets the preventive maintenance intervals and out-of-service
thresholds intended to reduce Mechanical Failure incidents across the BTA
bus and light rail fleet.

2. PREVENTIVE MAINTENANCE INTERVALS

Brake systems on all bus and light rail vehicles must be inspected every
6,000 miles of operation. Wheel and axle assemblies on light rail vehicles
must be inspected every 12,000 miles of operation. Door mechanisms on all
vehicle types must be inspected every 3,000 miles or every 60 days,
whichever comes first. Propulsion systems (engines on buses, traction
motors on light rail vehicles) are inspected every 15,000 miles.

3. OUT-OF-SERVICE THRESHOLDS

Any vehicle found with brake pad or shoe wear beyond 80 percent of its rated
service life must be pulled from service immediately and may not return to
revenue service until the brake component is replaced and reinspected. A
vehicle with a door mechanism that fails to fully close on 2 separate trips
in the same day must also be pulled from service immediately, regardless of
how recently it passed its scheduled door inspection.

4. COMMON CAUSES OF MECHANICAL FAILURE INCIDENTS

Based on the Authority's most recent annual maintenance review, Mechanical
Failure incidents break down by root cause as follows: brake system issues
account for approximately 38 percent of reported mechanical failures, door
mechanism issues account for approximately 22 percent, propulsion system
issues account for approximately 19 percent, and all other causes
(electrical, HVAC, and miscellaneous) account for the remaining 21 percent.

5. REPEAT-FAILURE INSPECTION RULE

Any single vehicle that is the subject of 2 or more Mechanical Failure
incident reports within a rolling 90-day period must undergo a full
inspection by a certified mechanic before it is returned to revenue
service, even if each individual failure was minor and the vehicle was not
otherwise due for scheduled maintenance.

6. PARTS AND DOCUMENTATION

Every brake and wheel/axle inspection must be logged with the routeId the
vehicle was most recently assigned to, the odometer reading at inspection,
and the inspecting mechanic's employee number. These logs are retained
alongside incident records under the Authority's standard 5-year retention
schedule.
